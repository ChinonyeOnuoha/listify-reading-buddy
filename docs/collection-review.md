### Content considerations and reading demands (language only; no age claim)
- **Whoop:** gentle; a trapped fish is frightened, then reassured; a short explainer on tides (the moon’s pull). Very short and simple, though StoryWeaver calls it Level 3. Place names: Mumbai.
- **Jackal and the Sun:** short sentences; a “find a wife” premise and notes about forced marriage; the artwork reworks paintings by San artists who have died (name them in About).
- **Kariza’s Questions:** germs and illness; a microscope scene.
- **Zebra:** slapstick (Baboon kicked and burned, “butt”); idioms and *biltong*.
- **A Fish and a Gift:** Afrikaans family words; a shark is netted and freed; a quarrel between fishers and surfers is mentioned.
- **Spring:** longest of the African stories; repeated structure helps; names *Nkanyezi, Ndlovu, Bhubezi*.
- **The Girl Who Could Not Stop Laughing:** fart and toilet jokes (the text lists them), an “explosion/bomb” metaphor for her laugh, sound-effect spellings that are fun but tricky to read aloud (“mmmbrrrpftbahahah…”), a Mahabharata reference (*Ghatotkacha*, a rakshasa), Indian words (*bonda, Anna, Sir*); the science (laughter, animals that laugh, face muscles) is simplified. Funny, and strongest as a read-aloud.
- **The Will:** the **longest and densest** (about 1,040 words, several pages of 150–200 words, with no picture on some of them); a father dies and his sons quarrel; religious terms (*Allah, vasiyat, naseehat*); family names *Abdullah, Rukaiya, Ahmad, Muhammad, Mukarram* (spelled “Mukkaram” once in the text); the arithmetic puzzle is the point and the mother’s solution is clever. If density is a concern, swap in *Sailing Ships* or *Raindrops*.
- **Sailing Ships (swap):** the most science, the longest with its four explainer pages; Tibetan words glossed in the book (*Genla, Tashi delek*); the physics explanation is simplified but sound.
- Pronunciation: the books give meanings for *Genla* and *Tashi delek* but no pronunciations, so **no pronunciation help is included**. Readability scores describe sentence and word length only and do not establish suitability for any age.
