# Accessibility notes

What the app does for accessibility, and what has and hasn't been verified. This is an engineering record, not a conformance claim: no screen-reader session on a real device, no audit tool and no user testing with disabled people has been done.

## Built in
- **Semantics and keyboard.** Real buttons, links, labelled inputs and headings throughout. Minute options and colour swatches are radio groups (`role="radio"` with `aria-checked`, and spoken names such as “10 minutes” and “Other, you choose the minutes”). Paste text / Photos of pages, Pick a story / Bring your own and Story / About this book are tab lists. Story filters and the reflection chips are toggle buttons (`aria-pressed`). Each screen's heading takes focus when the screen opens.
- **Focus visibility.** A solid 2 px amber outline with a 2 px offset for `:focus-visible` on every control, including Buddy and the playback slider.
- **One main action per screen**, at most one alternative beside it, and everything else in a labelled ⋯ menu (“More options”, “More for page 3”). Menus are Radix menus: arrow keys, Enter, Escape, focus returned to the trigger.
- **Dialogs and sheets** use Radix dialogs: focus moves in and stays inside, Escape closes, and focus returns to the control that opened it (for the story window, the cover that opened it; for a reflection, the chip that opened it). Confirmations that can't be undone are alert dialogs with Cancel focused first. Each has a visible title and, where it can be closed without a choice, a labelled Close button.
- **Undo instead of warnings.** Deleting a page, a recording, a session or a saved item is immediate and offers Undo in a polite status message for six seconds, so nothing has to be confirmed twice and a mistake can be reversed.
- **Photographed pages.** Thumbnails are a list box of options named “Page 1”, “Page 2, words added” and so on. Reordering never needs dragging: select a page, then Earlier or Later. Each move is announced (“Page 1 moved to position 2 of 3.”), as are added pages and saved words. Because a screen reader can't read the words in a photo, **Add the words on this page** stores them; they are read out and shown as resizable text under the photo while reading.
- **Page zoom viewer.** Zoom out, Zoom in and Fit page are labelled buttons in a labelled toolbar; one that cannot act is `aria-disabled` but stays focusable. The viewer is a focusable group named for the page (“Page 2 of 5”) with a hidden description of the keys: **+** / **−** zoom, **0** fits, **arrow keys** pan once zoomed. Button and key actions are announced politely.
- **Reading.** Passages and stories are real text that A− / A+ resize (18 to 32 px), in a 34em column with a 1.65 line height. Nothing on the reading page moves or highlights on its own. Page changes announce only “Page n of N”; focus stays on the button used, and moves to the other one at the first or last page.
- **Recording state** is announced through a polite live region and shown as text (“Recording”, “Paused”), never only as the lilac dot. Problems use `role="alert"`. The progress bar is a labelled `progressbar`. A short explanation comes before the browser's microphone question.
- **Playback** uses a labelled play/pause button and a native range input (“Playback position”, with the time as its value text), so it works with the keyboard and assistive technology.
- **Decorative art.** Buddy and the logo book are `aria-hidden` and not focusable; Buddy's interactive instances expose one button, “Say hello to your buddy”. The nook is one image with a text description that changes with what has been set (for example “The lamp is on and lights the armchair.”). Emoji in the reflection chips are hidden; the words carry the meaning.
- **Touch targets.** Buttons are 48 px tall and the main action 56 px; icon buttons 44 px; swatches 56 px; reflection chips 48 px.
- **Colour.** Text meets WCAG AA (4.5:1) on every surface it appears on, and control edges, selection rings and the recording dot meet 3:1. The numbers are in [design-system.md](design-system.md) and asserted by tests. Each colour has one meaning, and state is never shown by colour alone (selected swatches also get a ring and a bolder name; selected pages a ring; the selected feeling a ring and `aria-pressed`).
- **Motion.** Interface motion is short and eases out. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, dialogs, sheets and messages fade instead of moving or scaling, Buddy's waves and reactions are off, and the recording dot does not pulse.
- **Layout.** Containers keep their size as content grows (the page strip scrolls sideways inside the panel; the story window has one size for both views), so content never jumps. On narrow screens the main buttons stay at the bottom and messages appear under the header so they never cover them.
- **Sound** is off by default, never the only cue, and silent while recording or listening back.
- **Children's corner.** Stories use Andika, a face designed for early readers. Every picture has a short description written for this app, a book's picture-only pages stay picture-only, and pages printed after the story are labelled as such. A failed picture says so accurately and can be retried.

## Checked
In a headless Chromium at 390 × 844, 834 × 1194 and 1440 × 900: every screen of the main practice, the children's corner, the sample and Saved, including the page strip with eight pages, reordering, the page menu and Add the words, the story window's two views, recording with a simulated microphone, pause, finish, listen back, the reflection response, Undo, Delete all and a reload that restores the session. Automated tests cover contrast, Buddy's hidden markup and interaction, the zoom viewer, the story reader's focus and announcements, and the main flows (see [TESTING_LOG.md](../TESTING_LOG.md)).

## Not verified
Screen readers (VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA), Windows high-contrast/forced-colours mode, text-only zoom beyond browser zoom, switch or voice control, real-device touch behaviour (including dragging pages on touch screens), and a real microphone. Reduced-motion handling was verified by reading the CSS and scripts, not by watching the setting change in a browser.
