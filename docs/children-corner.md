# Children’s reading corner

A small, separate space inside Reading Buddy where a child can pick a short illustrated story (or bring one they love), read it aloud, and listen back. It is a **conceptual reading-practice prototype**: it does not assess, score or teach, it makes no claim about literacy, and nothing analyses a recording. Live AI feedback is not connected.

Related: [story manifest and review record](children-corner-content.md) · [user flow](user-flow.md) · [design system](design-system.md) · [accessibility](accessibility.md) · [architecture and privacy](architecture.md#privacy-and-network-behaviour).

## The flow
1. **A doorway** on the welcome screen: a compact horizontal invitation — small doorway drawing, “A doorway to stories”, “A reading corner for children” and a quiet text “Step inside →”. The whole invitation is **one** navigation control (one keyboard stop, no nested buttons or links); “Step inside” has no border, fill or permanent underline, a subtle hover and a clear focus ring. It enters the corner without any adult reading target and keeps both sessions. The reading-target card is centred on wide screens; “Just exploring? Try a sample session →” sits inside it beneath the target choices **at every width** (it is shown only once, as plain text with a comfortable tap height), and the doorway sits below and outside the card. **When the children’s session holds something real** (a chosen story, the child’s own text or pages, a recording) the same single control says so instead: “Back to your story”, the story’s title (or “Your own story”) and “Continue →”; if the child had only started preparing their own pages it reads “Continue your reading” / “Your pages are waiting”. Merely browsing the catalogue (opening and closing previews, filters, switching tabs) creates no indicator. Continuing just re-enters the same session — it creates nothing, restarts no recording and plays nothing — so the screen, story page, text size, recording and reflection come back as left. The adult session’s own “Resume session” card is separate and untouched; the doorway’s accessible name adds “in the children’s reading corner”. After an explicit discard (Exit session inside the corner) the plain invitation returns. The doorway picture is a **vector drawing made for this app** (`DoorwayArt.tsx`, re-using the real companion component). It replaces an earlier low-resolution crop of the approved concept image. **Outstanding:** a cleaner, painterly high-resolution original that matches the bookmark character — it has not been supplied, so the crop was removed rather than enlarged.
2. **Where shall we go today?** Two choices — *Pick a story* and *Bring your own* — in a segmented control. Picking shows a grid of illustrated covers: equal-height cards per row, a fixed picture area, titles that wrap in full (never truncated) and length lines aligned across the row. Columns fall back to fewer across at large text sizes and on narrow screens (`auto-fill`). Filters are *All*, *Folktales* and *Real world*. Selecting a cover opens a **story modal** (centred on wide screens; full-screen and opaque on phones, so no other story shows behind it): cover, title, a short description, length, **Read this story** (primary) and **About this story** (secondary). *About* swaps the content **inside the same modal** (author, illustrator, translator, licence and source links, with **Back to the story**) — dialogs never stack. One labelled **Close** control. Closing returns to the same card and scroll position; opening and closing never replace content, discard audio or start the microphone. When a recorded session would be replaced, *Read this story* asks first, inline.
3. **Bring your own** reuses the adult paste-text and upload-pages steps with child-friendly wording (“Bring a story you love. Paste a passage or add photos of your pages.”): the same two drafts, selected borders and checkmarks, page menu, **+ Add pages**, **Change content**, gallery and image zoom. Once content exists its preview takes priority and the large chooser collapses. There is no timed target.
4. **Read and record** reuses the proven recorder screen: start recording or upload audio → pause → *Listen so far* → resume into the same recording → finish → playback. Only the controls that apply are shown. On wide screens a compact panel sits beside the story; on phones a compact opaque dock stays anchored to the visible screen and the page reserves its height. There is no target, countdown or progress bar — just the state and the recorded time. For built-in stories the passage is real text with a picture (not text inside images), with **A−/A+** text-size controls (five steps; only the story text changes size) and a one-line “Page 1 of 12” counter. Uploaded pages stay in the zoomable viewer. The companion is still here. See *Steady page turns* below.
5. **You made time to read.** Playback (play/pause, seek, duration), then an optional reflection, **How was reading?** — *😊 Easy / 🙂 A bit hard / 🤔 Not sure* (visible labels; the emoji are hidden from screen readers). Choosing one highlights it and immediately opens a small response modal; nothing is revealed on the page itself, no score is shown and nothing navigates automatically:
   - **Easy** — “Ready for another?” · *Choose a story* / *Back to my recording*.
   - **A bit hard** — “That’s okay.” “Would you like a shorter story?” · *Find a shorter story* / *Back to my recording*.
   - **Not sure** — “That’s okay too.” “You can listen again or pick another story.” · *Listen again* / *Choose a story*.

   Each modal has one **Close** control. Closing keeps the reflection and the recording; the choice can be changed and the response reopened by selecting it again. It is session-only and optional. *Choose a story* opens the catalogue and keeps the recording. *Find a shorter story* opens the catalogue with a removable chip, “Shorter than ‘<current story>’” — it compares word counts only and never calls a story easier; with uploaded or own content it offers *Browse short stories* instead (the collection’s own “Short read” category, up to 420 words). If nothing is shorter it says so and offers the full collection. *Listen again* closes the modal and restarts the existing recording from the beginning after that explicit click (a visible message if the browser will not play it). *Back to my recording* returns focus to the player without playing. The chosen feeling also carries a small checkmark (never colour alone). Then **Choose another story**, **I’m done for now** and a quieter **Record again** (which asks before replacing the recording).

### Steady page turns
Story pages used to jump because each page had its own height and illustration. Now all pages of a story are stacked in one grid cell (inactive pages are invisible, `aria-hidden` and `inert`), so the reading area is as tall as the longest page and **the header, counter, previous/next and recording controls never move**. The illustration has a reserved slot (`clamp(11rem, 32vh, 20rem)`, full picture shown with its proportions kept) that exists before the image loads and stays when an image fails; the next and previous pictures are preloaded. There is no nested scroll area, no font shrinking and no height or sliding animation. After a turn the page scrolls only if the start of the new passage would be off-screen (never to the top of the app); focus stays on the button used and moves to the opposite button when that one disappears at the first or last page; only “Page N of M” is announced, never the passage. Page turns never pause, restart or discard a recording and never start playback; opening or closing *About* leaves the view where it was.

There are no scores, rankings, streaks, badges or claims about pronunciation, intelligence or ability, and the app does not say “well done” on the child’s behalf as a judgement.

## Sessions, navigation and privacy
- **Two separate sessions in the same tab.** The main (adult) session and the children’s session have their own recorder, content, recording and review state. Entering or leaving the corner never reads or writes the other session.
- **Home preserves.** The logo (or *I’m done for now*) returns to the main welcome screen and keeps both sessions. *Step inside* / *Continue* returns to exactly where the child left off — a finished recording opens in review; recording is never restarted.
- **Leaving mid-recording** explains that the recording will be finished and kept, and cancelling changes nothing. Browser Back does the same thing (it stays put and asks first).
- **Browser Back/Forward** move in and out of the corner (it has its own history entry); playback pauses on every navigation and never autoplays on return.
- **Exit session** is the only way to discard, and it states its scope: inside the corner it clears only the children’s session (and says the main session isn’t affected); on the main screens it clears only the main session (and says the children’s session isn’t affected). Cancelling changes nothing; focus starts on the safe choice.
- **Choosing another story** keeps the recording (“Your recording of … is saved for now. Listen back”). Starting a *different* reading replaces it only after a clear choice (*Replace and read* / *Keep my recording*).
- **Privacy:** stories and covers are served from the app itself — no third-party requests are added. Everything the child does (choices, recordings, own pages, reflection) stays in this tab’s memory and is never uploaded. No accounts, names, birth dates, public sharing, analytics, profiles or paid services; the microphone is requested only after *Start recording*. Closing or reloading the tab clears everything.

## Who it is for
The audience is a **design hypothesis, not a validated claim**: independent readers around 8–12, as briefed. Measuring the eight stories supports a narrower, lower suggestion:

- Their Flesch–Kincaid grade is about 3.8–7.0 (median 4.7) — short sentences, familiar words. That points to children who can read short paragraphs on their own, **roughly 7–10 years old**, as the core fit.
- **11–12-year-olds** will probably find most of the collection easy; the *Real world* titles and *Hare and Tortoise (Again!)* are the most demanding. A collection for that range would need longer, richer texts than these.
- **Younger children (about 5–7)** may enjoy the stories read aloud with an adult but are unlikely to read them independently.
- These are readability measures of the text only. They say nothing about themes, interest, or any individual child, and no children have tried the corner. Suitability needs review by educators or parents and testing with children.

## What it does not do
No live feedback of any kind; no transcription; no reading-level tracking; no stored history; no offline pictures (the story text is part of the app’s scripts, but the pictures are fetched when a page is shown); no sound effects; no audio of the stories themselves.

## Where things live
- `src/components/children/` — the corner’s screens, the doorway and the separate session hook.
- `src/content/stories/` — manifest, loaders and the eight text modules (loaded one at a time, only when a story opens).
- `public/stories/<slug>/` — optimised pictures (loaded lazily, page by page). The doorway is an inline SVG (`DoorwayArt.tsx`), not an image file.
